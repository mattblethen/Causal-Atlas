# Black Loyalists

## Function
Black Loyalists were enslaved and free Black people who supported, joined, worked with, or fled to British forces during the Revolution, often seeking freedom from Patriot slaveholders. They were not simply British instruments: many used imperial military promises to pursue survival, mobility, family preservation, and freedom.

## Priority Stack
1. Seek freedom and survival.
2. Exploit wartime openings created by British military policy.
3. Navigate danger from Patriot retaliation, British uncertainty, disease, and displacement.

## Internal Tensions
British offers of freedom were strategic, uneven, and often bounded by Loyalist property claims. The Book of Negroes shows both the power and fragility of the promise: many Black Loyalists were recorded for evacuation, while some fugitives were returned or remained vulnerable to re-enslavement.

## Research Notes
Keep this actor note centered on Black agency. British policy created openings, but Black Loyalists made decisions under extreme constraint and danger.
