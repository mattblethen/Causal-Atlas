# Source - Colonial Williamsburg Sons and Daughters of Liberty

## Bibliographic Info
Colonial Williamsburg article by historian Benjamin L. Carp on the Sons of Liberty and broader liberty mobilization.

## Useful Claims / Evidence
- The Sons of Liberty spread news through pamphlets, newspapers, tavern readings, and correspondence.
- The term "Sons of Liberty" became associated with organized protest against the Stamp Act by early 1766.
- Women participated in liberty mobilization as Daughters of Liberty, including spinning meetings in Providence and Norwich in 1766.
- Abstaining from tea and imported luxuries made domestic labor and consumption part of constitutional protest.
- In late 1768, Daughters of Liberty responded to the Townshend duties with new resolutions against tea.
