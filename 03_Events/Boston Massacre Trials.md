# 1770 - Boston Massacre Trials

## Summary
The Boston Massacre trials converted a political street crisis into courtroom evidence, legal argument, verdicts, and public legitimacy claims. They show that revolutionary escalation in Boston still operated through colonial legal process before open war.

## What Happened
After the March 5, 1770 shootings, Captain Thomas Preston and eight soldiers were tried in Boston. Preston was acquitted in October. In the soldiers' trial, known in the Adams Papers as Rex v. Wemms, John Adams and Josiah Quincy argued for the defense while the court weighed testimony about crowd violence, self-defense, and individual responsibility. On December 5, 1770, the jury acquitted six soldiers and found Matthew Killroy and Hugh Montgomery guilty of manslaughter rather than murder; both were later allowed benefit of clergy and branded in the hand.
