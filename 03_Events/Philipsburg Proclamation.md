# 1779-06-30 - Philipsburg Proclamation

## Summary
The Philipsburg Proclamation expanded British wartime freedom policy for people enslaved by rebels. It made Black flight to British lines part of imperial military strategy and deepened the contradiction between Patriot liberty claims and Patriot slaveholding.

## What Happened
British commander Sir Henry Clinton issued the proclamation from Philipsburg in New York on June 30, 1779. Unlike Dunmore's 1775 offer, which tied freedom to military service in Virginia, Clinton's policy promised security to people enslaved by rebels who reached British lines and chose any occupation there. The policy excluded people enslaved by Loyalists, but it widened the practical path for Black Loyalists and freedom seekers across the war zone.

- Patriot slaveholders treated British freedom policy as both military threat and property loss.
