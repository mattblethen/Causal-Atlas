# 1767 - Townshend Revenue Act

## Summary
The Townshend Revenue Act imposed import duties on selected goods entering British American colonies and dedicated the revenue to imperial administration, justice, civil government, and colonial defense. It made the taxation conflict reappear through customs duties after the Stamp Act repeal.

## What Happened
Parliament passed a revenue statute in 1767 that placed duties on goods including glass, lead, painters' colors, paper, and tea imported from Great Britain into the colonies. The act treated the money as imperial revenue and authorized its use to support colonial civil government and the administration of justice. It also tightened customs reporting, penalties, and writs-of-assistance powers.
