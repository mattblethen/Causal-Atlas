# 1783 - Black Loyalist Evacuation

## Summary
The Black Loyalist evacuation from New York turned British wartime freedom promises into a contested postwar migration. The Book of Negroes recorded thousands of Black people leaving under British protection while American claimants contested property and treaty obligations.

## What Happened
As the British evacuated New York in 1783, Sir Guy Carleton refused to return many formerly enslaved people who had reached British lines before the preliminary peace agreement. British officials recorded Black evacuees in the Book of Negroes, noting names, ages, origins, prior legal status, former enslavers, ships, and destinations. Many sailed to Nova Scotia and what became New Brunswick, while some cases remained contested and some fugitives were returned.

## Related Decisions
- Sir Guy Carleton chose to honor British freedom policy for many people who had reached British lines before the peace cutoff.
- British officials chose to document evacuees in order to manage American complaints and possible compensation claims.
- Black Loyalists chose evacuation despite uncertainty, racism, poverty, family separation, and the risk of re-enslavement.
