# 1776-01-10 - Common Sense

## Summary
Common Sense was Thomas Paine's January 1776 pamphlet arguing for independence and against monarchy. It made separation from Britain more publicly thinkable by translating elite constitutional conflict into a forceful popular print argument.

## What Happened
Published anonymously in Philadelphia by Robert Bell on January 10, 1776, Common Sense appeared after Congress's reconciliation appeal had failed and after the king had treated resistance as rebellion. The LOC title-page record anchors the Bell imprint, while the American Battlefield Trust provides a direct primary-text transcription of Paine's argument.

The pamphlet's form mattered as much as its claims. Clements Library and the Smithsonian show Common Sense as a rapid print-circulation event: a first edition of roughly 1,000 copies, repeated editions, printer disputes, and twenty-five print editions within a year. Washington's April 1776 letter to Joseph Reed gives contemporary evidence that the pamphlet was changing opinion in Virginia.
