# 1775-07-06 - Declaration of the Causes and Necessity of Taking Up Arms

## Summary
The Declaration of the Causes and Necessity of Taking Up Arms was Congress's public justification for armed resistance. It paired military defense with continued reconciliation language, making it the companion document to the Olive Branch Petition.

## What Happened
The Second Continental Congress adopted the declaration in Philadelphia on July 6, 1775, after Lexington and Concord and amid the Boston siege crisis. The text rehearsed longstanding constitutional grievances, treated British military action as coercive violence, and argued that resistance by force had become necessary. At the same time, it insisted Congress did not yet mean to dissolve the imperial union or establish independent states.

Founders Online's editorial note treats the final document as a collaborative result of Thomas Jefferson's draft and John Dickinson's revision, rather than a simple radical/moderate split. The National Park Service similarly emphasizes that Dickinson, Jefferson, and other delegates shaped a declaration that balanced armed defense with hopes for reconciliation.
