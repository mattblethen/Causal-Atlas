# Decision Log - American Revolution

## 1765 - Parliament chooses direct colonial taxation

Decision:
Parliament imposed stamp duties inside the colonies to raise revenue connected to imperial defense and administration.

Why it mattered:
This made postwar debt pressure visible as a constitutional dispute over taxation and representation.

Priority conflict:
- British fiscal control versus colonial consent and local autonomy.

Alternatives:
- Request funds through colonial assemblies.
- Reduce imperial military commitments.
- Rely more heavily on external trade regulation.

Consequences:
- [[Stamp Act]]
- [[Colonial Resistance]]

- [[Source - American Battlefield Trust Stamp Act 1765]]

## 1773 - Parliament preserves tea duty while aiding the East India Company

Decision:
Parliament changed tea policy in a way that helped the East India Company while leaving the disputed tea duty in place.

Why it mattered:
The policy made colonists confront both monopoly trade and parliamentary taxation.

Priority conflict:
- Imperial commercial management versus colonial political economy and resistance to taxed tea.

Alternatives:
- Repeal the tea duty.
- Let the East India Company absorb losses.
- Negotiate colonial acceptance through assemblies.

Consequences:
- [[Tea Act]]
- [[Boston Tea Party]]

Sources:
- [[Source - UK National Archives Boston Tea Party]]

## 1774 - Parliament chooses coercion after the Boston Tea Party

Decision:
Parliament punished Boston and Massachusetts through the Coercive Acts.

Why it mattered:
The response made many colonists see the Massachusetts crisis as a threat to colonial rights more generally.

Priority conflict:
- Imperial authority and order versus colonial self-government and solidarity.

Alternatives:
- Prosecute individuals only.
- Negotiate compensation for tea.
- Repeal or revise disputed tea policy.

Consequences:
- [[Coercive Acts]]
- [[First Continental Congress]]

Sources:
- [[Source - UK National Archives Boston Tea Party]]
- [[Source - Bill of Rights Institute Coercive Acts]]

## 1775 - Gage sends troops toward Concord

Decision:
General Thomas Gage sent British regulars to seize and destroy arms and supplies at Concord.

Why it mattered:
Military enforcement turned political resistance into open armed conflict.

Priority conflict:
- Imperial security and disarmament versus colonial militia readiness.

Alternatives:
- Delay military action.
- Negotiate with Massachusetts leaders.
- Target political leaders rather than supplies.

Consequences:
- [[Lexington and Concord]]

Sources:
- [[Source - NPS April 19 1775]]
- [[Source - NPS Rebellion Minute Man]]

## 1775 - Congress creates continental military command

Decision:
Congress created a Continental Army and appointed George Washington as Commander in Chief.

Why it mattered:
The decision converted local and provincial armed resistance into a continental military institution.

Priority conflict:
- Local militia autonomy versus the need for unified command.

Alternatives:
- Continue relying mostly on provincial militia.
- Appoint a New England commander rather than a Virginian.
- Delay continental military organization while pursuing reconciliation.

Consequences:
- [[Continental Army]]
- [[George Washington]]
- [[Olive Branch Petition]]

Sources:
- [[Source - NPS Washington Appointment Commander in Chief]]

## 1775 - Dunmore offers freedom to enslaved people held by rebels

Decision:
Lord Dunmore declared martial law in Virginia and offered freedom to enslaved people held by rebels if they joined British forces.

Why it mattered:
The decision weaponized the contradiction between Patriot liberty claims and slavery, while opening a dangerous but real route toward freedom for enslaved people.

Priority conflict:
- British military strategy and royal authority versus Patriot slaveholding society and enslaved people's pursuit of freedom.

Alternatives:
- Avoid emancipation policy and rely on conventional military manpower.
- Offer broader emancipation beyond rebel-held enslaved people.
- Negotiate with Patriot slaveholders rather than threatening their labor system.

Consequences:
- [[Lord Dunmore's Proclamation]]
- [[Black Loyalists]]
- [[Thread - Slavery and American Contradiction]]

Sources:
- [[Source - American Battlefield Trust Lord Dunmore's Proclamation]]

## 1776 - Congress adopts independence

Decision:
The Second Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence.

Why it mattered:
The colonies moved from seeking redress inside the empire to claiming separation from it.

Priority conflict:
- Reconciliation and imperial membership versus sovereignty and self-government.

Alternatives:
- Continue petitioning for reconciliation.
- Remain in armed resistance without declaring independence.
- Seek negotiated dominion-style autonomy.

Consequences:
- [[Declaration of Independence]]
